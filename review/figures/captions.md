## figure1.png (fig:abstract)

Three routes to one estimate of the power spectral density (PSD) of eight seconds of one channel of de-identified scalp EEG at 128~Hz from the authors' archive ($N=1024$; one epoch), with design resolution $R=8/(N\Delta)=1$~Hz ($\alpha=4$). (a)~The multitaper estimate with all $N$ Slepian tapers weighted by their eigenvalues $\lambda_i$ (thick blue), the untapered periodogram smoothed with a box of width $R$ (green, dashed), and the average of the periodograms of a sinc window of bandwidth $R$ and length $N_s=16N$ slid across the record one sample at a time (pink, dotted). (b)~Absolute differences from the multitaper estimate: the smoothed periodogram agrees to $3\times10^{-13}$~dB and the sliding-window average to $0.06$~dB. (c)~The maximum difference of the sliding-window average against the window length $N_s$, with a $1/N_s$ guide line (dotted). The multitaper estimate here has $\nu=17$ equivalent degrees of freedom, as defined in Section~\ref{sec:periodogram}.

## figure2.png (fig:motif)

Every estimator is the true spectrum blurred by a kernel, plus noise. One realization ($N=1024$) of an autoregressive process whose PSD (black) has two peaks $7/(N\Delta)$ wide at half power and $30/(N\Delta)$ apart on a background spanning 65~dB. (a)~Periodogram estimate. (b)~Periodogram of the record after a 25\% cosine taper. (c)~The same, smoothed with a parabola whose half-power width $R=8/(N\Delta)$ is that of a peak. (d)~Smoothed with $R=48/(N\Delta)$, which exceeds the separation of the peaks. The equivalent degrees of freedom are $\nu=2$, 2, 16.7 and 98.4. (e--h)~The expected-value kernel of each estimator in dB relative to its peak, centered at $f\Delta=0.4$. Where the side lobes are too close to draw, the curve is the envelope of their peaks. The zoomed-in view of the main lobe is shown on the top left. The half-power widths are 0.8, 1.2, 8.2 and $47.8/(N\Delta)$, and 1.3\%, 0.02\%, 0.12\% and 65\% of the kernel's power lies outside $|f|\le8/(N\Delta)$, our measure of broadband leakage. The skirts of the Fej\'er kernel fall slowly, so power leaks from the peaks into the region 40~dB below them (a,\,e). The cosine taper lowers the skirts by 22~dB at $16/(N\Delta)$ and by 39~dB at $40/(N\Delta)$, and widens the main lobe by 15\% (f). A kernel as wide as a peak is just right (g), and a wider kernel merges the peaks (h).

## figure3.png (fig:sweep)

Choosing the resolution. (a)~The record of Fig.~\ref{fig:motif}, $N=1024$ samples of the AR(4) process (black), the 25\% cosine taper scaled to the record's peak amplitude (dashed), and the tapered record (orange). (b)~Root-mean-square error over frequency, in dB, of the cosine-tapered periodogram smoothed with a parabola of half-power width $R$, against the true PSD, averaged over 300 independent realizations; the curves are ensemble averages and carry no error bars. The noise (the standard deviation of the estimate) falls with $R$, the bias (blur by the kernel) grows, and their total over the whole band (solid black) has a broad minimum, which sits at smaller $R$ when only the peak region, $0.09\le f\Delta\le0.16$, is scored (dashed). The circled points are panels (b), (c) and (d) of Fig.~\ref{fig:motif}, and the dotted lines mark $R$ equal to the width of a peak and to the separation of the peaks.

## figure4.png (fig:slepian)

Why the multitaper kernel is a box ($N=256$, $R=8/(N\Delta)$). (a)~The first, second and seventh Slepian tapers. (b)~The kernels $|H^{(i)}(f)|^2/\Delta$ of the first three, each filling the gaps left by the previous ones within the band $|f|\le R/2$ (dotted lines). (c)~The running sum $\sum_{i\le L}\lambda_i|H^{(i)}(f)|^2/2\alpha\Delta$ as tapers are added, darker for larger $L$. With all $N$ tapers it is \emph{exactly} the box of width $R$ convolved with Fej\'er's kernel, which is the identity \eqref{eq:identity} in kernel form and cannot be distinguished from the box itself (dashed) at this scale. With the usual $L=2\alpha-1=7$ it is the box with rounded shoulders and small ripple. (d)~The concentration ladder: the fraction $1-\lambda_i$ of each taper's energy outside the band. Tapers beyond the $2\alpha$th carry only leakage.

## figure5.png (fig:banks)

Three ways to build a bank of tapers from one window, and the reference bank ($N=256$, $R=8/(N\Delta)$). (a--d)~The bank as constructed, in one colour for each route, with lighter shades for later members of the bank in (b--d). (a)~Welch's method with Hann segments of length $N/4$ at 50\% overlap: seven time-shifted copies of one window. (b)~A periodogram tapered with a 25\% cosine taper and smoothed with a parabola of half-power width $R$: frequency-shifted copies of one taper, four of a continuum. (c)~A sinc window of bandwidth $R$ slid across the record one sample at a time, four positions shown; in the limit of a long window this is exactly the untapered periodogram smoothed with a box, and exactly Thomson's estimate with all $N$ tapers weighted by $\lambda_i$. (d)~Thomson's estimate with $L=7$ equally weighted Slepian tapers, the first four shown. (e--h)~The eigen-weight ladder $c_i$ of each, normalized to unit sum, whose flatness sets $\nu=2(\sum_ic_i)^2/\sum_ic_i^2$ by \eqref{eq:ladder}: $\nu=13.4$, 16.7, 17.2 and 14.0. The eigen-tapers of (c) are the Slepian sequences themselves. Fig.~S4 adds the eigen-tapers and kernels.

## figure6.png (fig:everyday)

The estimators used in practice, applied to the record of Fig.~\ref{fig:motif} ($N=1024$, $R=8/(N\Delta)$; one realization; true PSD black). (a)~The untapered periodogram smoothed with a box, which is the exact estimate \eqref{eq:identity} ($\nu=17.2$). (b)~Thomson's estimate with $L=7$ tapers ($\nu=14.0$). (c)~A cosine-tapered periodogram smoothed with a parabola of the same half-power width ($\nu=16.7$). (d)~Welch's method with nine Hann segments of 192 samples stepped by 104, chosen for about the same half-power width ($\nu=17.6$). The four half-power widths are $8.2$, $7.2$, $8.2$ and $7.8/(N\Delta)$. This realization has more leakage than is typical. Its median absolute errors over the band are 11.6, 4.6, 0.9 and 0.8~dB, and the values over 300 realizations are 5.3, 2.3, 1.0 and 1.0~dB (Table~\ref{tab:ensemble}). (e--h)~The kernels, as in Fig.~\ref{fig:motif}, with 1.4\%, 0.22\%, 0.12\% and 0.48\% of their power outside $|f|\le R$. The untapered box (e) inherits the Fej\'er tails, and its estimate levels off 20~dB above the true spectrum. The seventh Slepian taper puts the skirt of the multitaper kernel at $-22$~dB (f), and that estimate levels off 10~dB above the true spectrum. The kernel of the cosine-tapered parabola (g) has no side lobes and falls below $-38$~dB beyond $1.5R$. Welch's kernel (h) has no flat top, and its side lobes are spaced at the scale of the peaks.

## figure7.png (fig:eeg8s)

The estimators used in practice agree to within their own noise. (a)~The EEG epoch of Fig.~\ref{fig:abstract} estimated by Thomson's method with $L=7$ tapers ($\nu=14$, blue), by a cosine-tapered periodogram smoothed with a parabola of half-power width $R$, recipe (b$'$) ($\nu=16.7$, orange), and by Welch's method with nine Hann segments of $1.5$~s stepped by $0.81$~s ($\nu=17.6$, pink). Their half-power widths are $0.91$, $1.03$ and $0.97$~Hz. (b)~Differences from the multitaper estimate: median absolute difference $0.6$~dB (smoothed) and $0.9$~dB (averaged), 95th percentiles $4.2$ and $5.9$~dB. The medians are smaller than the estimators' own noise ($4.3\sqrt{2/\nu}$ gives $1.6$, $1.5$ and $1.5$~dB). The largest differences are in the valleys between harmonics, which the side lobes of the seventh Slepian taper fill in.

## figure8.png (fig:eeg)

Three estimators of a seizure. (a)~Ten minutes of de-identified scalp EEG at 200~Hz (ELROND seizure-annotation set), channel C4 against the common average, displayed between $\pm500~\mu$V; the seizure is shaded, and the triangles, like the dashed lines in the spectrograms, mark the instants used in (d) and (f). (b)~Six seconds of the ictal rhythm from $t=318$~s. ## figure9.png (fig:sleep)

Sleep spindles at three resolutions. (a)~Forty seconds of stage N2 sleep, channel C4-M1, filtered between 0.3 and 35~Hz and between 11 and 16~Hz (offset), with five spindles shaded. (b--g)~Spectrograms from windows of 1~s (b,\,c), 2~s (d,\,e) and 4~s (f,\,g) stepped by 0.1~s, by the multitaper estimate with $L=2\alpha-1$ tapers, at least one (left), and by a cosine-tapered periodogram smoothed with a parabola (right), on one colour scale. The white box is the window length by the achieved half-power width, so it shows the resolution of the panel, and twice its area is approximately $\nu$. (h)~Achieved half-power widths and equivalent degrees of freedom for multitaper (blue circles) and smoothing (orange squares) at each setting. At 1~s, $\alpha=1$ and the practical multitaper rule gives $L=1$, so its achieved width is 1.1~Hz against 2.1~Hz for smoothing; at $\alpha=2$ the widths are much closer. Short windows keep the spindles brief and blur them in frequency, and long windows do the opposite.

Kernels at the same design resolution ($N=256$, $R=8/(N\Delta)$; Table~2), (a)~on a linear scale near the main lobe and (b)~in dB relative to the peak: the ideal box (dotted); the $L=7$ multitaper kernel ($\nu=14.0$, blue); the periodogram after a 25\% cosine taper followed by a parabola of the same half-power width ($\nu=16.7$, orange), which is recipe (b$'$); and the untapered ($\nu=17.2$, green), Hann ($\nu=8.9$, olive) and Bohman ($\nu=7.3$, light blue, dashed) periodograms followed by the box. Dropping the leakiest taper. ## efigure3.png (fig:tradeoff)

Variance, leakage and side-lobe level against the measured resolution, for $N=256$. All curves are exact kernel and degrees-of-freedom computations, with no sampling variability. Each method's bandwidth parameter is swept and the quantities are plotted against the resulting half-power width, so methods are compared at equal resolution: multitaper with $L=2\alpha-1$ and $2\alpha-2$ Slepian tapers and with sine tapers; untapered, Hann and Bohman periodograms followed by a box; a cosine-tapered periodogram followed by a parabola; a Hann periodogram followed by a Gaussian; Welch's method with 50\% overlap. (a)~Equivalent degrees of freedom $\nu$ (higher means less variance). (b)~Kernel power beyond twice the half-power half-width. (c)~The peak of the kernel beyond $1.5\times$ the half-power half-width. The Gaussian, parabolic and Welch kernels have no flat top, so for them this quantity is the skirt of the main lobe and not a side lobe. The dotted line marks the $8/(N\Delta)$ width of Table~2, and the legend is at the lower right.
