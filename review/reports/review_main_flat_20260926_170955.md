## SUMMARY
A rhetorically strong, well-storied unification paper whose main VSNC weakness is that the **introduction carries no News** (no numbers, no contributions list) and the abstract announces results with hedged verbs ("we show", "we give", "explain") instead of the strong ones it has earned ("we prove", "we demonstrate", "we test").

## VSNC — Abstract

**Vision ✅**
The big idea is stated in six words and in the right place: *"We show that the three are the same estimator."* The empowerment promise is present and concrete: *"We give a recipe for all three routes"* and *"explain what balance of blur against noise makes a bandwidth 'just right.'"* A reader knows what they will be able to do afterwards.

Minor improvement: state the *practical* payoff of the unification (why a reader should care that they're the same). Add one clause:

> "…are one estimator. Practitioners may therefore use whichever route their pipeline already computes — including frequency-dependent bandwidths and two-axis smoothing of spectrograms, which are awkward to express in tapers — with a known, quantified cost in variance."

**Steps ✅**
Unusually good for an abstract. The execution is enumerated in one sentence: *"the three methods build the bank from one window in three ways: by shifting it in time, by shifting it in frequency, or by taking orthogonal tapers."* That is the whole recipe in a breath.

**News ⚠️**
The abstract *promises* quantification but withholds the numbers: *"Three practical facts follow, each quantified"* … *"a single taper costs about a third of the degrees of freedom"* … *"can beat the equal-weight multitaper estimate."* "About a third" and "can beat" are the vaguest formulations of your strongest results. Maximum specificity is available — you have $\nu = 8.9$ vs $14.0$, 38% vs 47% relative standard error, median errors 1.3 dB vs 4.6 dB, agreement to $3\times10^{-13}$ dB, EEG agreement of 1.5 dB, band powers within 0.7 dB. Put four of those in the abstract.

Concrete replacement for the "three practical facts" sentence:

> "Three practical facts follow, each quantified. Smoothing or averaging cannot remove leakage: the box-smoothed untapered periodogram floors 20 dB above a spectrum spanning 65 dB (median error 11.6 dB) while the same box on a Hann-tapered periodogram tracks it to 1.3 dB. A single taper costs about a third of the degrees of freedom of the $K$-taper estimate ($\nu=8.9$ against $14.0$ at $N=256$, $NW=4$; 38% against 47% relative standard error), because its bank has unequal weights. And the last Slepian taper leaks 6% of its energy, putting the multitaper kernel's skirt at $-22$ dB against the Hann kernel's $-36$ dB, so the Hann-tapered, box-smoothed periodogram beats the equal-weight multitaper estimate on the same record (median error 1.3 dB against 4.6 dB)."

Also promote the machine-precision number out of Fig. 1's caption into the abstract: *"the two agree to $3\times10^{-13}$ dB on real EEG."* And the EEG result is currently *"agree to within their own noise"* — give the number: *"agree to a median of 1.5 dB over all spectrogram pixels, and band powers to within 0.7 dB."*

**Contributions ⚠️**
The verbs are weak relative to what you did. You **prove** identity (8.3) in Appendix A; you **demonstrate** the sliding-sinc limit with measured convergence; you **implement and test** everything in a repository; you **identify** the eigen-weight ladder as the mechanism of the variance cost. The abstract says only "We show", "We give", "show on EEG", "explain", "Code … is provided."

Suggested verb upgrades:
- "We show that the three are the same estimator" → **"We prove that the three are one estimator."**
- "We give a recipe for all three routes, show on EEG that they agree…" → **"We present a recipe for all three routes, demonstrate on ten-minute seizure EEG that they agree to within their own noise, and identify the eigen-weight ladder — the eigenvalue spectrum of the estimator's quadratic form — as the quantity that determines the variance of any of them."**
- "Code reproducing every figure is provided." → **"We implement all three routes and both identities in a tested Python package that reproduces every figure."**

## VSNC — Introduction

**Vision ✅**
*"This paper shows that the three methods are one method (Fig. 1)."* Placed at the head of paragraph 2, exactly where it belongs. The vision is also framed against a named pain point in paragraph 1: *"many users experience it as a black box"* / *"its parameters … do not map onto the intuitions users bring from the first two methods."* That is a textbook problem statement.

Missing: the empowerment promise in the reader's own terms. Add at the end of paragraph 2:

> "A reader who finishes this paper will be able to set a multitaper bandwidth from the width of the narrowest feature that matters, predict the degrees of freedom and the skirt height of any of the three estimators before running it, and choose among them on grounds of convenience rather than folklore."

**Steps ✅**
The roadmap paragraph enumerates the steps cleanly: *"Section 2 sets up the one picture … Section 3 presents averaging, smoothing and tapering … Section 5 shows that the three are one …"* Good. One addition: say what the *method* of the paper is, not just its order — "every estimator is written as a quadratic form, diagonalized, and compared through its kernel and its eigen-weight ladder" — so the reader knows the machinery before Section 5.1 introduces it.

**News ❌**
This is the real gap. The introduction contains **no result numbers at all**. The only quantities are $NW$, $K$, and "equation (8.3)". Everything quantitative lives in figure captions and Section 5.4. A reader who reads only the introduction learns the claim but not a single piece of evidence.

Add a News paragraph immediately before the roadmap sentence:

> "The consequences are quantitative throughout. At $N=256$ and $W=4/N$, the box-smoothed untapered periodogram and the eigenvalue-weighted all-taper estimate are the same estimator to machine precision and carry $\nu=17.2$ degrees of freedom; the standard $K=7$ estimate carries $14.0$ and the Hann-tapered, box-smoothed periodogram $8.9$, a third fewer. On an AR(4) spectrum spanning 65 dB the median errors are 11.6 dB (box alone), 4.6 dB ($K=7$), 1.3 dB (Hann then box) and 0.8 dB (Welch). On ten minutes of seizure EEG the multitaper and smoothed-periodogram spectrograms differ by a median of 1.5 dB, the size of their own noise, and band powers by less than 0.7 dB."

**Contributions ⚠️**
The contributions *are* enumerated — but in Section 7, page 10: *"What we add is the account for users: the three methods as one bank built three ways, the ladder as the explanation of variance cost, the sliding-window form of the identity, exact bookkeeping per route, the bandwidth rule, and the recipe validated on EEG."* That is an excellent contributions list in the wrong place, and phrased with the weakest possible verb ("add"). Copy it into the introduction as a bulleted or verb-led list and keep the Section 7 version for the prior-work comparison:

> "We make five contributions. We **prove** that averaging periodograms, smoothing a periodogram and multitaper estimation are one estimator, a bank of tapers with weights built three ways from one window. We **derive** the sliding-window form of Thomson's identity (8.3) and **identify** the Slepian sequences as the principal components of a sinc window slid across the record. We **identify** the eigen-weight ladder as the quantity that sets the variance of any quadratic estimator, and **show** that the variance cost of the single-taper route follows from its slope and from nothing specific to Slepian sequences. We **compute** exact kernels, leakage and degrees of freedom for all seven estimators at matched bandwidth (Table 1). We **present** a recipe for each route and **test** it on scalp EEG containing focal seizures."

Pick **"Three routes, one estimate"** as the slogan and **"a bank of tapers with weights"** as its gloss, and use exactly those words every time — title, abstract, Fig. 1, §5.1, §6 heading (already "three routes to one estimate" ✅), conclusion. **Salient ⚠️**
The standout idea is clear: identity (8.3) makes the multitaper estimate *exactly* a box-smoothed periodogram. But §5.4 presents **four** "things worth stating plainly," and each is itself a named finding ("Smoothing and averaging cannot undo leakage," "A single taper costs variance," "The last Slepian taper leaks," "Short segments cost kernel shape"). Four co-equal bold statements compete with each other and with the identity. Rank them: the abstract already picks three; promote *"the last Slepian taper leaks enough that a Hann-tapered, box-smoothed periodogram can beat the equal-weight multitaper estimate"* to primary status (it is the surprising one) and demote "short segments cost kernel shape" to a sentence inside the Welch discussion. **Surprise ✅**

**Story ✅**

## 1. Problem stated clearly and early? — **A–**

The problem lands in sentence one of §1 and is sharpened by the end of the first paragraph:

> "Yet its parameters, a time--bandwidth product $NW$ and a number of tapers $K$, do not map onto the intuitions users bring from the first two methods"

This is a *real* problem, stated concretely, in the right place. Two small defects:

- The abstract's problem statement is buried inside a 350-word single-paragraph block. A reader skimming gets "Three ways of estimating a power spectrum are in everyday use" — a topic, not a problem.
- "many users experience it as a black box" is an unsupported assertion doing load-bearing work. Either evidence it or make it structural rather than psychological.

**Suggested rewrite (abstract opening):**
> "Users of multitaper spectral estimation must choose a time--bandwidth product $NW$ and a taper count $K$, parameters with no counterpart in the two methods everyone learns first — averaging periodograms and smoothing a periodogram. We show the three methods are the same construction, so that the multitaper parameters can be read off from quantities users already understand."

**Suggested rewrite (§1, replace the black-box clause):**
> "Yet its two parameters have no counterpart in the first two methods: nothing in 'average the segments' or 'smooth across frequency' tells a user what $NW=4$ buys, or what is lost by taking $K=7$ tapers rather than 6."

## 2. Reader told WHY they should care? — **B**

The significance is *present but back-loaded and understated*. The strongest motivation in the paper is the finding that a standard pipeline choice is badly wrong — and it appears on page 6:

> "where the truth is at $-20$~dB it floors near $0$~dB ... its median error is 12~dB"

and

> "for $N=128$, $NW=3$: 12~dB median error with $K=5$, 6~dB with $K=4$"

A 12 dB error from a default setting is the "why care." It should be in the intro, not just in §5.4. Right now the intro's stakes paragraph is the weakest in the paper:

> "The practical question then is whether one can simply smooth, or average. The answer is ``yes, if you taper first, and it costs some variance,'' and the paper makes both clauses quantitative."

That is a methodological convenience question, and it undersells. Worse, the conclusion actively deflates the stakes:

> "For most EEG work any of the three is fine, and knowing they are the same is what lets one choose on grounds of convenience."

**Suggested rewrite (add to §1 after the "three routes, one estimate" paragraph):**
> "The unification is not only conceptual. It predicts, and we confirm, that two common defaults fail on spectra with the dynamic range of neural recordings: smoothing an *untapered* periodogram floors 20 dB above the truth (median error 12 dB on our test spectrum) because smoothing provably cannot cancel leakage; and the standard choice $K=2NW-1$ retains a taper leaky enough that the multitaper estimate floors 10 dB high — 6 dB worse than a Hann-tapered, box-smoothed periodogram computed in three lines. Knowing the three estimators are one construction is what makes both failures predictable rather than surprising."

Also rescue the conclusion's last sentence:
> "...knowing they are the same is what lets one choose on grounds of convenience — and knowing *how* they differ is what keeps a default from costing 10 dB."

## 3. Prior work surveyed and critiqued — clear WHY unsatisfactory? — **B+**

Survey coverage is excellent (§7 is a model of scholarly honesty: Thomson, Percival–Walden, Riedel–Sidorenko, Walden, Nuttall–Carter, Hansson-Sandsten, Abreu–Romero, Karnik et al., Bronez). The *critique* is thinner than the survey. The stated gap is essentially "nobody told the users":

> "None of this has reached the tutorials neuroscientists read \cite{babadi2014,prerau2017} or the textbook account that begins with averaging \cite{kay1988}."

That is a legitimate gap, but it is asserted rather than demonstrated, and it is the entire critique. Strengthen by naming *what the missing knowledge causes*: e.g. that the tutorials give no rule for choosing $W$ beyond convention, and give no basis for predicting the $K=2NW-1$ leakage floor.

**A real internal tension to fix.** The abstract claims:

> "An identity that Thomson stated in 1982 but that has gone almost unnoticed"

but §7 states:

> "Karnik, Romberg and Davenport use \eqref{eq:identity} in matrix form for a fast multitaper algorithm \cite{karnik2022}."

A 2022 *IEEE Trans. IT* paper using the identity is not "almost unnoticed." Scope the claim to the audience you actually mean.

**Suggested rewrite:** "An identity that Thomson stated in 1982, exploited since in the theory literature (e.g. \cite{karnik2022}) but absent from the tutorials and textbooks practitioners read, makes one correspondence exact."

## 4. New approach introduced in the intro? — **A**

Fully satisfied. The mechanism is given in the intro, in plain words, before any mathematics:

> "Averaging periodograms builds the bank by shifting one window along the record; smoothing builds it by shifting one taper along the frequency axis; Thomson builds it from orthogonal tapers."

This is the best sentence in the paper. Fig. 1 as a graphical abstract with quantified agreement ($3\times10^{-13}$ dB) is exactly right.

One gap: the *contribution list* exists but is hidden in §7 ("What we add is the account for users: the three methods as one bank built three ways, the ladder..."). Move that clause verbatim into the intro as a short enumerated list, and leave §7 to do the attribution.

## 5. Clear why this work is better, and in what specific ways? — **A–**

Specificity is exemplary: every claim carries a number ($\nu=8.9$ vs $14.0$; $-22$ dB vs $-36$ dB; $0.27$–$0.29$ vs $0.86$ Frobenius; median differences of $1.6$ dB against $4.3\sqrt{2/\nu}=1.6$ dB). Table 1 and Table 2 are the kind of exact bookkeeping reviewers rarely get.

The one weakness is **claim calibration in the title and abstract**, which the body then walks back:

> Title: "Averaging Periodograms, Smoothing a Periodogram, and Multitaper Estimation Are One Estimator"
> Abstract: "We show that the three are the same estimator."
> §5.4: "These remove the leakage in three different ways and are therefore close, not identical."
> §5.4: "Multitaper estimation is the box-smoothed periodogram ... with its leakage subtracted."

What is actually true, and still interesting: all three are members of one two-parameter family (quadratic forms = weighted taper banks); *one specific triple* coincides exactly; the everyday members differ in known, quantified ways. The strong headline will draw a "this is false" from any signal-processing reviewer who reads §5.4 first.

**Suggested title:** "One Estimator, Three Constructions: Averaging, Smoothing, and Multitaper Spectrum Estimation" — or, if you want the honest version, "Averaging Periodograms, Smoothing a Periodogram, and Multitaper Estimation Are the Same Taper Bank Built Three Ways."

**Suggested abstract edit:** "We show that the three are one construction — a bank of tapers with weights — and that one particular triple of settings gives *literally the same numbers*, while the everyday settings differ in ways we quantify exactly."

> "half a page in a late section that few readers reach, and mentioned in neither tutorial"

Reads as a mild scold of Babadi & Brown and Prerau et al. Suggest: "half a page in a late section, and not carried forward into the tutorial literature."

> "with a taper, as good on leakage and a third short on degrees of freedom; without one, wrong."

"Wrong" is punchy but absolute — it's wrong *for high-dynamic-range spectra*, which the paper itself notes is where it matters ("this bias is zero for a flat spectrum"). Suggest "without one, unusable on any spectrum with real dynamic range."

That is how it should be done.

**Ending: GOOD — no future-work section.** The paper ends on a substantive conclusion that restates the result and its consequence, not on a list of things the authors might do someday. This is the correct ending and should be preserved. One caveat: because there is no future-work section *and* no limitations paragraph, the paper never states its scope boundaries. Add two sentences (not a section) to the conclusion: adaptive weighting is not analysed beyond citation; coherence/multivariate estimators and non-stationary records are outside scope; the EEG demonstration is two clips and one channel each.

## STRUCTURAL–CONCEPTUAL DIAGNOSIS

- **Eq. (9) silently changes "=" to "≈" one paragraph after the boxed exact identity.** The text says "Rearranging (8)," which cannot produce an approximation. ## 1. Positioning (Abstract / Introduction / §7 / §8)

**What works.** The Adelson structure is largely satisfied: the intro states the problem (three estimators, one of them a black box), and §7–§8 confirm delivery with specifics (identity, ladder, sliding-sinc form, recipe, EEG validation). Comparisons are *quantitative* (Table 1, Frobenius distances, dB errors), not "ours is better." §7's final paragraph ("What we add…") is exactly the right move: it enumerates the delta rather than asserting novelty.

**Findings.**

1. **The novelty framing around Thomson (8.3) is the most attackable sentence in the paper.** "An identity that Thomson stated in 1982 but that has gone almost unnoticed" will draw a reviewer who knows Percival & Walden. The equivalence between eigenvalue-weighted all-taper multitaper estimates and Daniell/lag-window smoothing, and more generally between lag-window and multitaper estimators, is treated in P&W (and in Walden/McCoy/Percival's work on effective bandwidth). *Suggestion:* replace "gone almost unnoticed" with a precise claim of the form "stated in a late section of \cite{thomson1982}, discussed in the statistical literature as a relation between lag-window and multitaper estimators (\cite{percival1993}, §; Walden–McCoy–Percival 1995), used algorithmically by \cite{karnik2022}, and absent from the tutorials read by neuroscientists \cite{babadi2014,prerau2017}." You lose nothing: your contribution is the three-way bank picture, the ladder, the sliding-sinc form and the recipe, not priority on (8.3).

2. **The title/abstract claim "the three are one estimator" is stronger than what §5.4 and Appendix C.2 actually support.** C.2 concedes that a *tapered-then-smoothed* periodogram is not the average of any single sliding window (Q not Toeplitz), and §5.4 shows the everyday trio are merely close. The defensible claim is: all three live in the same family (quadratic forms = taper banks), and there is one exact triple within it. *Suggestion:* add one explicit sentence to the Conclusion drawing this line ("identity for the exact trio; membership in one family, with measured distances, for the everyday trio"), so a reviewer cannot frame it as overclaim.

3. **Comparison against a strawman.** The claim that "a Hann-tapered, box-smoothed periodogram can beat the equal-weight multitaper estimate" is made against equal weights at $K=2NW-1$. You already name the two standard fixes (adaptive weighting, $K=2NW-2$) but never evaluate the first. A reviewer will say: "compare to Thomson adaptive weights, which were designed for this." *Suggestion:* add an adaptive-weight row to Table 1 and an adaptive-weight trace to Fig. 8 / Fig. S2, and restate the claim as "with the default equal-weight, $K=2NW-1$ settings shipped by common toolboxes"—which is both true and more interesting, since that *is* what pipelines run.

4. **Missing engagement with the one paper whose conclusion competes with your recipe.** Riedel–Sidorenko–Thomson \cite{riedel1994} found "a few Slepian tapers then smoothing" best; this is currently a one-clause aside at the end of §5.4. If their conclusion stands, the honest recommendation is a fourth route. *Suggestion:* devote a short paragraph to it—either add "(d) a few Slepians then smooth" to the everyday trio with its own $\nu$/kernel row, or explain why you exclude it (no intuitive single-window story?).

5. **Hansson-Sandsten comparison should be quantitative.** She already approximates the Thomson estimator by a Welch structure; you claim the sliding sinc is the window that makes it exact in the limit. That invites: how does her window differ, and by how much (same Frobenius metric you already use in `fit_single_window.py`)? One number closes this.

## 3. Limitations — **absent; must be added**

There is currently no paragraph anywhere that states what the paper does not establish. Specifics a reviewer will supply if you do not:

- **Everything rests on stationarity within the analysis window and on Gaussianity for the $\chi^2_\nu$ bookkeeping.** EEG spectrograms at 2 s in a seizure chirp are explicitly nonstationary—the chirp *is* drift within the window. Say so, and note that within-window nonstationarity broadens all three kernels in the same way (which is probably why they agree).
- **What the smoothing route cannot do.** You mention Thomson's F-test in §6 but not the other multitaper-specific machinery: per-taper replicates for jackknife variance/confidence intervals and for jackknifed coherence, cross-spectra and multivariate/coherence estimation, and adaptive (data-dependent) weighting. - **Validation scope.** Two ten-minute clips, one channel each, one AR(4) process. "Agree to within their own noise" is a weak criterion: agreement in median |difference| is consistent with a shared bias, and the estimators are not independent, so the noise-scale comparison is heuristic. State the criterion's limits explicitly.
- **Failure modes of your own recipe.** Where does Hann-then-box fail? (iv) The exact trio in §6 is the *leaky* estimate, which no one should use—a reader could mis-implement the recipe; consider a one-line warning inside the framed box.
- **The "just right" bandwidth rule is calibrated on one synthetic spectrum.** Fig. 3's optimum drifting from $4/N$ to $8/N$ depending on the scoring region is itself evidence that the rule is soft; own that.

## 2. Cross-Cutting Themes

**Theme A — The central claim is asserted with the wrong calibration in both directions.** The abstract hedges verbs it has earned ("we show," "we give") while §8.3 over-claims ("an identity that Thomson stated in 1982 but that has gone almost unnoticed" — a sentence Percival & Walden readers will attack). One consistent editorial policy — *strong verbs for what is proved, precise scoping for what is prior* — fixes all of these.

**Theme B — Verifiability lags behind precision.** The paper quotes remarkably precise numbers (3×10⁻¹³ dB, 0.06 dB, 3.5% @ 2N, 0.17% @ 32N, per-figure median errors), but the two EEG datasets and `demos/fit_single_window.py` are absent and those specific figures cannot be re-derived. Precision without a reproducible path is a reviewer magnet, especially in a paper whose selling point is "we provide code reproducing every figure."

**Theme C — The contribution is enumerated everywhere except where readers look for it.** §8 ("What we add…") does an excellent job; the abstract does a good job; the *introduction* has no News, no numbers, and no contributions list. Related: the abstract's identity is delivered in one 90-word sentence, and the roadmap paragraph is fused to the motivating question. Readers who skim meet a topic, not a claim.

**Theme D — Presentation defects cluster where the novel claims live.** §5.4's fitting experiment is buried, §7's prior-work sentences use clausal subjects that postpone the verb, and the EEG figures/Table 2 report no uncertainty or sample size. The three most novel or most contestable claims are the three least well-presented.
