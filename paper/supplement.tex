Figure, table and equation numbers without an ``S'' refer to the main text, whose notation (Section~II-A there) is used throughout: $R$ is the design resolution, $\alpha=N\Delta R/2$ the time--bandwidth product, $L$ the number of tapers, and widths are in units of $1/(N\Delta)$.

\begin{figure}[!htb]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig1_kernels.png}
\caption{Kernels at the same design resolution ($N=256$, $R=8/(N\Delta)$; Table~II), (a)~on a linear scale near the main lobe and (b)~in dB relative to the peak: the ideal box (dotted); the $L=7$ multitaper kernel ($\nu=14.0$, black); the periodogram after a 25\% cosine taper followed by a parabola of the same half-power width ($\nu=16.7$, red), which is recipe (b$'$); and the untapered ($\nu=17.2$, green), Hann ($\nu=8.9$, cyan) and Bohman ($\nu=7.3$, olive, dashed) periodograms followed by the box. All main lobes have nearly the same half-power width, and the skirts differ by 40~dB.}
\label{fig:kernels}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[!htb]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{fig2_ar4_leakage.png}
\caption{Dropping the leakiest taper. (a)~One realization of the AR(4) process ($N=1024$, $R=8/(N\Delta)$): the periodogram ($\nu=2$, grey); the untapered periodogram smoothed with the box ($\nu=17.2$, green); the equal-weight multitaper estimate with $L=7$ ($\nu=14$, black) and with $L=6$ ($\nu=12$, cyan), and the adaptively weighted estimate with $L=7$ (olive); the cosine-tapered periodogram smoothed with a parabola of half-power width $R$ ($\nu=16.7$, red); and the true PSD (blue, dashed). (b)~Each smoothed estimate minus the true PSD: the untapered box floors 20~dB high, $L=7$ floors 10~dB high, dropping the seventh taper halves that, and the adaptive weights and the smoothed periodogram track the truth.}
\label{fig:ar4}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[!htb]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig3_tradeoff.png}
\caption{Variance, leakage and side-lobe level against the measured resolution, for $N=256$. All curves are exact kernel and degrees-of-freedom computations, with no sampling variability. Each method's bandwidth parameter is swept and the quantities are plotted against the resulting half-power width, so methods are compared at equal resolution: multitaper with $L=2\alpha-1$ and $2\alpha-2$ Slepian tapers and with sine tapers; untapered, Hann and Bohman periodograms followed by a box; a cosine-tapered periodogram followed by a parabola; a Hann periodogram followed by a Gaussian; Welch's method with 50\% overlap. (a)~Equivalent degrees of freedom $\nu$ (higher means less variance). (b)~Kernel power beyond twice the half-power half-width. (c)~The peak of the kernel beyond $1.5\times$ the half-power half-width. The Gaussian, parabolic and Welch kernels have no flat top, so for them this quantity is the skirt of the main lobe and not a side lobe. The dotted line marks the $8/(N\Delta)$ width of Table~II, and the legend is at the lower right.}
\label{fig:tradeoff}
\end{figure}
